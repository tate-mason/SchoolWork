# Paper 1 - "Lifetime Earnings and the Vietnam Era Draft Lottery: Evidence from Social Security Administrative Records" - Joshua D. Angrist (1990)

## Question 1: What is the research Question

How did being in the Vietnam war affect labor outcomes, specifically earnings? They induce randomization via the draft lottery, implying there was no endogeneity since people were selected, ostensibly, at random.

## Question 2: Why is it important to answer such a question?

This is an important question for both moral and political reasons. Morally, it is important to know if soldiers are fairly compensated after their service. There should not be a penalty for service, and thus understanding how being a soldier impacts labor outcomes is important. Policy-wise, understanding labor market outcomes is important when implementing legislation     which is targeted at soldiers and veterans. 

## Question 3: What are the key endogeneity concerns?

Endogeneity concerns lie in health status as well as education status. For instance, healthy individuals are likely to be eligible, while those in college could likely defer service. Thus, units being healthier or more educated, two covariates which impact earning potential, could be endogenous to the treatment.

## Question 4: What is the argument for the relevance of the IV of the paper? Does the paper show any evidence in favor of that?

The paper argues that the draft lottery is relevant because it is correlated with serving in Vietnam. 

## Question 5: What is the argument for the validity of the IV of the paper? Does the paper show any evidence in favor of that?

The paper argues that the draft lottery is valid because it is random, and thus uncorrelated with earnings directly, only through the channel of serving in Vietnam.

## Question 6: What is the argument for the monotonicity of the IV of the paper? Does the paper show any evidence in favor of that?

There are no defiers here, as while one can defy the draft, one cannot defy the lottery. The assumption is reasonable given the nature of the instrument and thus does not require argumentation.

## Question 8: Can you criticize the validity and the monotonicity assumptions? What are the most realistic violations of these two assumptions you can think of?

Monotonicity is quickly violated by draft dodgers and those who enlist, a group they discuss themselves. Validity is more difficult to violate here, due to the random nature of being drafted.

## Question 9: Related to question 8, can you think of additional evidence the paper could have shown to check whether your criticisms are important for the conclusions of the paper?

The paper could have provided more evidence in favor of the validity of the instrument by showing that the draft lottery is uncorrelated with other covariates that may impact earnings. Further, there could have been more discussion on the impact of draft dodgers and those who enlist, and how these groups may impact the results.

## Question 10: What is the strongest part of the paper in your view?

The strongest part of the paper is the use of the draft lottery as an instrument. The draft lottery is a well-known natural experiment, and its random nature makes it a strong candidate for an instrument. Additionally, the paper does a good job of addressing potential endogeneity concerns and providing evidence in favor of the relevance and validity of the instrument.

## Question 11: What is the weakest part of the paper in your view?

The weakest part of the paper is the lack of discussion on potential violations of the monotonicity assumption. While the paper does mention draft dodgers and those who enlist, it does not provide any evidence or discussion on how these groups may impact the results. Additionally, the paper could have provided more evidence in favor of the validity of the instrument, such as showing that the draft lottery is uncorrelated with other covariates that may impact earnings.

# Paper 3 - "Children and Their Parents' Labor Supply: Evidence from Exogenous Variation in Family Size" - Joshua D. Angrist and William N. Evans (1998)

## Question 1: What is the Research Question

The authors seek to understand the impact of family size on parental labor supply. They use sex composition of the first two children as instruments, arguing that these factors are random and thus uncorrelated with other factors that may impact parental labor supply.

This is an important question for labor economics and family economics. Understanding the impact of family size on parental labor supply is crucial for policymakers to make informed decisions about family policy, such as childcare subsidies and parental leave policies, especially for poorer families. Additionally, understanding the impact of family size on parental labor supply is important for understanding the trade-offs that parents face when making decisions about work and family.

## Question 3: What are the key endogeneity concerns?

The key endogeneity concern is that family size may be correlated with labor preferences which in turn impact labor supply. For example, parents who prefer to work less may choose to have more children.

# Paper 4 - "Using Geographic Variation in College Proximity to Estimate the Return to Schooling" - David Card (1993)

## Question 1: What is the Research Question

The author seeks to understand the impact of college proximity on educational attainment and earnings. He uses geographic variation in college proximity as an instrument, arguing that those who live closer to colleges are more likely to attend college, which in turn impacts their educational attainment and earnings.

## Question 2: Why is it important to answer such a question?

This is an important question because it informs policy decisions regarding college access and affordability. Understanding the impact of college proximity on educational attainment and earnings is crucial for policymakers to make informed decisions about education policy, such as funding for public colleges and universities.

## Question 3: What are the key endogeneity concerns?

Key endogeneity concerns include college proximity being correlated with familial factors like parental education, which could encourage both living near a college and higher educational attainment. Another concern is that college proximity could be correlated with local labor market conditions, which could impact earnings. However, the author controls for this and makes the argument with mainly the lowest income quartile, which should alleviate this concern. Further, there is an issue of educational quality in primary schooling, which could be correlated with college proximity and impact educational attainment and earnings.

## Question 4: What is the argument for the relevance of the IV of the paper? Does the paper show any evidence in favor of that?

The author argues that college proximity is relevant because it is correlated with college attendance, which in turn impacts educational attainment and through that channel, earnings. The paper provides evidence in favor of this argument by showing that proximity to a college is positively correlated with earnings outcomes, especially for those in the lowest income quartile.

## Question 5: What is the argument for the validity of the IV of the paper? Does the paper show any evidence in favor of that?

The author argues that college proximity is valid because it is random and thus uncorrelated with other factors that may impact educational attainment and earnings. The paper acknowledges that it relies on restrictive assumptions for validity, but argues that controlling for local labor market conditions and focusing on the lowest income quartile helps to alleviate concerns about correlation with other factors.

College proximity is monotonic because defying the instrument would not be observable. If someone lived near a college and chose not to go, we would not know if they were a defier or just had other reasons for not attending. The assumption is reasonable given the nature of the instrument.
