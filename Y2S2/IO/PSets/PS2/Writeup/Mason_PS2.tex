\section{Introduction}

This problem set was concerned with evaluating models of entry and competition. Given data from Jia (2008), I used a variety of estimation techniques to estimate the probability of entry. First, I used a probit, then a different specification of the probit, and finally an IV model to model entry simply. Next, I used an ordered probit \'a la Bresnahan and Reiss (1991) to model the effect of number of firms in a market on entry. Lastly, I used the Seim (2006) method to model the effect of predicted entry of a competitor on own entry.

This report is laid out as follows. Section 2 will present the profit and entry functions, section 3 will present the simple estimation results, section 4 will present the ordered probit results, and section 5 will present the Seim method results. Finally, section 6 will conclude.

\section{Profit and Entry Functions}

The profit function for Walmart is as follows:

\begin{equation*}
  \pi_{t}^W(N_t) = \underbrace{R_t(N_t)\bar{\mu}_t}_{\text{Net revenue}} - \underbrace{[\sum_i\sum_lD_{it}(N_t)\Omega_{il}(N_t)sh_{il}(N_t)]}_{\text{Shipping cost}} - \underbrace{\sum_l L(q_{lt})w_{lt}}_{\text{Labor cost}} - \underbrace{\sum_lF_{lt}}_{\text{Fixed cost}}
\end{equation*}

Where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $D_it = \frac{R_{it}(N_t)}{\bar{p}_t}$ is orders from county $i$
  \item $\bar{mu}_t$ is the average markup in the market
  \item $\Omega_{il}(N_t)$ is the order flow matrix
  \item $sh_{il}(N_t)$ is the cost of shipping from location $l$ to county $i$
  \item $q_{lt} = \sum_i\Omega_{il}(N_t)D_it(N_t)$ is the orders fulfilled by location $l$
\end{itemize}

The profit function for KMart is as follows:

\begin{equation*}
  \pi_{t}^K(N_t) = \underbrace{R_t(N_t)\bar{\mu}_t}_{\text{Net revenue}} - \\
  \underbrace{[\sum_i\sum_lD_{it}(N_t)\Omega_{il}(N_t)sh_{il}(N_t)]}_{\text{Shipping cost}} \\
  \underbrace{\sum_l L(q_{lt})w_{lt}}_{\text{Labor cost}} -  \\
  \underbrace{\sum_lF_{lt}}_{\text{Fixed cost}}
\end{equation*}

Where all follows as above

The entry function for Walmart is as follows:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbbm{P}(\text{Entry})^W = \max_a\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\rho_t\beta)^t[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_t^{K}}(\pi_{jt}^g - c_{jt}^g - \tau d_{jt}^g) + \sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}^{0}}(\pi_{jt}^f - c_{jt}^f - \tau d_{jt}^f)]
\end{equation}

Note, in this equation, Walmart is choosing to enter either a market $g, \ f$ which already has a KMart, or a market with no firms. Entry is determined by profit net of costs in each location, discounted by a productivity adjusted discount factor.

The entry function for KMart is as follows:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbbm{P}(\text{Entry})^K = \max_a\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\rho_t\beta)^t[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_t^{W}}(\pi_{jt}^g - c_{jt}^g - \tau d_{jt}^g) + \sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}^{0}}(\pi_{jt}^f - c_{jt}^f - \tau d_{jt}^f)]
\end{equation}

\subsection{Comparing Across Specifications}
In the simple case, it would seem we are biasing the results upwards by not including competition effects, as the presence of a competitor should act as a deterrent. When we include competition effects, we see that the constant term becomes more negative, which is more consistent with the idea that there are significant barriers to entry, and the presence of a competitor is a significant deterrent to entry. The IV results show that the presence of a competitor is an even stronger deterrent than the OLS results suggest, which implies that there is likely some endogeneity in the OLS results, as firms are more likely to enter into markets where there are no competitors, and less likely to enter into markets where there are competitors. This effect is especially pronounced for KMart, who sees Walmart's potential entry as a strong deterrent.

% ── 6. Conclusion ───────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

In conclusion, this problem set was concerned with evaluating models of entry and competition. Going from the simplest possible probit model of entry to a (simplified) method which accounts for strategic planning of entry, we see that the effect of a competitor on the entry decision is significant, and likely much stronger than can be fully captured in a simple probit. Comparing across models, one thing is constant: a competitor entering the market is a deterrent to entry. What differs is the magnitude of the effect. In the probits, this is a very small effect, with the IV having the strongest at $-4.58$ for predicted Walmart entry on KMart. When we move to the ordered probit, we see that the first mover is greatly incentivized to enter, but after that, the gain from entry is much smaller, consistent with the idea that being first to market provides great value. Finally, the Seim method shows that Walmart's predicted entry is hugely influential on KMart's entry decision, but KMart's predicted entry may actually slightly encourage Walmart to enter the market, though this may be a bug. 

In all, I think the ordered probit and Seim methods are more consistent with the idea of strategic entry, and would be used well in tandem to model the effect of competition on entry. The simple probit methods are useful for baseline estimation, but the true effect of entry competition is likely stronger than can be captured.
