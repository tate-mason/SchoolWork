Organized by theme. Stars (★) mark papers with clean causal identification strategies most relevant as methodological models or direct citations.

## 1. Role Models in Sports — Direct Participation Effects

**Ahlert & Doering (2014)**
"Role models in sports – Can success in professional sports increase the demand for amateur sport participation?"
*Sport Management Review* 17(3): 324–336.
Survey of 1,413 German amateur soccer players. Past success of national teams increased demand only modestly; hypothetical present success has larger effects. Availability, relevance, and similarity to the role model are the main predictors.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1441352313000533

**Sport Scotland (year varies)**
"Sporting Success, Role Models and Participation: A Policy Related Review"
Policy-oriented review concluding that evidence for sustained participation impacts from elite sporting success alone is limited.
https://sportscotland.org.uk/media/sxxh0rff/sportingsuccessrolemodelsandparticipation.pdf

**Descheemaeker et al. (2025)**
"The trickle-up effect: exploring the relationship between youth sports participation and elite sporting success"
*Managing Sport and Leisure* 30(6).
23 years of data across 29 sports in Flanders. Modest positive correlation (r = 0.15) between youth membership and elite success; substantial sport-by-sport heterogeneity. Raises identification concerns — trickle-down rarely automatic.
https://shura.shu.ac.uk/34913/1/Descheemaeker%20et%20al%20(2025)%20The%20Trickle%20Up%20Effect.pdf (open access)
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/23750472.2025.2459729

**Weed et al. (ResearchGate)**
"The Olympic Games and raising sport participation: A systematic review of evidence and an interrogation of policy for a demonstration effect"
Systematic review finding mixed evidence for Olympic trickle-down; highlights that leveraging initiatives (local investment) are needed for effects to materialize.
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274265826

**Sheffield Hallam / PMC**
"Winning Olympic medals associated with increased physical activity in hometown youth"
Identification strategy: geographic proximity — hometown youth show stronger responses when medal winners are locally connected (shared coaches, facilities, programs).
https://www.shu.ac.uk/news/all-articles/latest-news/winning-olympic-medals-associated-with-increased-physical-activity-in-hometown-youth

## 2. Title IX — Causal Evidence on Girls' Sports Participation ★

★ **Stevenson (2010)**
"Beyond the Classroom: Using Title IX to Measure the Return to High School Sports"
*Review of Economics and Statistics* 92(2): 284–301.
**IV strategy**: pre-Title IX boys' state-level participation rates instrument for the change in girls' participation 1972–1978. 10 pp rise in female sports participation → 1 pp increase in college attendance, 1–2 pp rise in labor force participation; effects on male-dominated occupations.
https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article-abstract/92/2/284/58583/Beyond-the-Classroom-Using-Title-IX-to-Measure-the
NBER WP: https://www.nber.org/papers/w15728
SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1548806

★ **Kaestner & Xu (2010)**
"Title IX, Girls' Sports Participation, and Adult Female Physical Activity and Weight"
*Evaluation Review* (Sage). 20 pp increase in female participation → 3 pp decline in obesity probability. Exploits same Title IX variation as Stevenson.
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0193841X09353539

**Anderson (NBER WP w12113)**
"Effects of Title IX and Sports Participation on Girls' Physical Activity and Weight"
NBER Working Paper 12113.
https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12113/w12113.pdf

## 3. Female Role Models — Causal Evidence Outside Sports (Analogies) ★

★ **Carrell, Page & West (2010)**
"Sex and Science: How Professor Gender Perpetuates the Gender Gap"
*Quarterly Journal of Economics* 125(3): 1101–1144.
**Random assignment** of introductory math/science professors at USAFA. Female professors eradicate the gender gap in follow-on STEM course-taking among high-ability female students. Clean causal design — most cited role model paper in economics.
https://faculty.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/scarrell/gender.pdf
NBER: https://www.nber.org/papers/w14959

★ **Breda, Jouini, Napp & Thebault (2023)**
"How Effective are Female Role Models in Steering Girls Towards STEM? Evidence from French High Schools"
*The Economic Journal* 133(653): 1773–...
Large-scale **field experiment** — brief classroom exposure to female scientists shifts high-achieving girls toward male-dominated STEM programs in college (CS, engineering). Effect strongest in fields with severe underrepresentation.
https://academic.oup.com/ej/article-abstract/133/653/1773/7055938
Open: https://shs.hal.science/halshs-01713068/document

**Porter & Serra (2020)**
"Gender Differences in the Choice of Major: The Importance of Female Role Models"
*American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*.
Successful female economists as role models → 8 pp increase in women choosing economics from a 9 pp baseline.
https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20180426

## 7. Related: Jackie Robinson Effect / Visibility of Minority Pioneers

**Anzia & Berry (2011)**
"The Jackie (and Jill) Robinson Effect: Why Do Congresswomen Outperform Congressmen?"
*American Journal of Political Science* 55(3).
Mechanism: when a group faces discrimination, only the most qualified members succeed — visible pioneers therefore set a higher bar, inspiring followers. Useful theoretical framing for female athlete role models.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-5907.2011.00512.x
PDF: https://gspp.berkeley.edu/assets/uploads/research/pdf/Anzia_Berry_8_5_09.pdf

## 8. Do Sports Participation Cause Outcomes? (Endogeneity / Selection)

**Cabane, Hille & Lechner (2018)**
"Do high school sports build or reveal character? Bounding causal estimates of sports participation"
*Economics of Education Review* 64: 75–89.
Uses bounds approach to address selection into sports participation; distinguishes building vs. revealing mechanisms. Good methodological reference for the endogeneity problem.
https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecoedu/v64y2018icp75-89.html

## Notes on Identification

Most causal evidence in this literature uses:
- **IV / natural experiment**: Title IX variation across states × cohorts (Stevenson 2010, Kaestner & Xu 2010)
- **Random assignment**: professor gender assignment (Carrell et al. 2010)
- **Field experiment**: role model classroom visits (Breda et al. 2023)
- **Geographic proximity**: hometown/local connection to athlete (Sheffield Hallam)
- **Diff-in-diff**: pre/post exposure to women's league or athlete entry

The gap in the literature is clean causal identification of **professional women's sports league presence** (e.g., WNBA market entry/expansion) on **girls' youth sport participation** — DiD exploiting staggered expansion is underexplored.
